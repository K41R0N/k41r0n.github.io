# Pass 1: Big Idea

**Purpose:** Identify the single idea that anchors the copy. A strong idea gives the reader a reason to continue and can support more than one expression without becoming vague.

**When to use:** Before writing the draft.

## How to find it

### 1. Read the brief

Confirm the topic, angle, format, audience and goal. If the brief is missing or unclear, stop and ask for what is needed.

### 2. State the core message

Ask: "What is the one thing I want the reader to remember after reading this?" Write a single sentence. It should state a meaningful benefit or idea, not merely praise the organization or product.

### 3. Test it

- **Clarity:** Can it be stated in one sentence, preferably 15 words or fewer?
- **Reader relevance:** Would the intended reader care?
- **Distinctiveness:** Is there a substantiated reason it differs from alternatives?
- **Fit:** Does it match the brief and approved positioning?
- **Durability:** Could it support more than one piece without losing its point?

If any answer is no, revise before continuing. Do not rely on an unsupported superlative, promise or comparative claim to make the idea sound distinctive.

## Output

Put the one-sentence idea at the top of the draft. Every section should support it or be removed.

## Common mistakes

- **Multiple ideas:** choose one; give supporting points subordinate roles.
- **Vagueness:** replace praise with a specific reader benefit and a verified reason to believe it.
- **Brief mismatch:** ensure the idea answers the brief, not a neighboring topic.
- **Unsupported difference:** do not turn an unverified claim into the central idea.
