# Pass 3: Voice Matching

**Purpose:** Make the copy sound like the intended speaker addressing the intended reader, rather than generic marketing or a mismatched audience register.

**When to use:** After factual anchoring. Resolve material unsupported claims first.

## Required reads

1. The current voice guide, including its register and approved or disallowed wording.
2. The project's voice principles, if maintained separately.
3. The brief and any approved audience profile.
4. The project's writing mechanics and editing guidance.

If a required reference is missing, record the gap. Do not invent a voice rule.

## The rule

Define the reader from the brief: what they know, need and are deciding. Read the draft aloud. If the language sounds generic, patronizing, overly familiar or too formal for that reader, revise it.

Aim for clarity and specificity. Explain project-specific terms when readers may not know them; do not explain familiar basics without a reason. Use the approved tagline only after verifying its current wording. Prefer direct address when it fits the voice.

## How to match voice

1. Read the current register and vocabulary guidance before editing.
2. Remove generic phrases and filler. Replace them with specific, supported information.
3. Check whether the copy over-explains familiar concepts or assumes unexplained project terminology.
4. Check distance and familiarity: avoid needless institutional phrasing and insider slang.
5. Read aloud and apply the reader test: is it precise, respectful and worth the reader's attention?
6. Keep the voice judgment separate from claim-honesty and legal review. They answer different questions.

## Common voice mistakes

- Empty phrases that sound polished but say little.
- Motivational language that adds no information.
- Unneeded hedges or confident wording that overstates the evidence.
- Over-explaining basics or leaving essential terms unexplained.
- Register drift toward formality or in-group slang.

## Output

A revised draft that follows the project's current voice guidance and fits the reader defined in the brief.
