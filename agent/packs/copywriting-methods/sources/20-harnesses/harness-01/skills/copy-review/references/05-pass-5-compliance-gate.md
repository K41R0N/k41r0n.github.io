# Pass 5: Final Review

**Purpose:** Check terminology, substantiation, legal review triggers, quotations and approvals before submission. This checklist is a general review method, not project-specific legal advice.

**When to use:** After story structure is complete and before submitting a draft for approval.

## The rule

Do not submit copy with unresolved failures. Fix the issue or route it to the qualified reviewer. A clean automated check is not a legal review or approval.

## Final checklist

### 1. Terminology and claims

- Check the current project terminology and claims guidance.
- Use financial, technical or regulated terms only with their approved meaning and context.
- Do not imply a legal or regulatory status without current approved substantiation.
- Do not rely on a past claim or wording as proof that it is allowed now.
- If the needed guide is missing, flag the gap; do not invent a ban or approved alternative.

### 2. Legal review triggers

Route the draft through the current project process when it includes legal or regulatory interpretations, claims about risk or performance, forward-looking statements, commitments, or statements about relationships with other entities. Qualified reviewers determine the applicable requirements. Do not assert that a phrase or structure is legally safe based on this method.

### 3. Context and substantiation

Check whether claims could imply services, guarantees, control or outcomes that the product does not provide. Compare each statement with current approved product facts and legal guidance. For short formats, identify where the reader can find essential context. Strong comparisons and superlatives need current evidence and any required review.

### 4. Claim-honesty test

Ask: "Can we substantiate and stand behind every sentence?" Check accuracy, clarity and restraint. Keep this distinct from voice matching: a claim can be accurate but sound wrong, or sound right but lack support.

### 5. Quotes and third parties

Verify quotations against their original source and preserve context. Never invent or silently paraphrase a quote as verbatim. Obtain required permission before using a quote or speaking for another person or organization. Treat outside organizations as independent unless an approved source establishes otherwise.

### 6. Numbers and time-sensitive details

Re-check figures, dates, rates and other changing facts against current approved sources. Record the source and an as-of time where useful. Do not publish a stale figure simply because it appeared in an earlier draft.

### 7. Approval and submission

Follow the current project approval process. Submit the draft with its central idea, source notes, unresolved questions and format-specific needs. Do not send or publish it until required approval is recorded.

## Output

A draft with unresolved issues fixed or clearly routed, traceable sources, and the required review state. A clean check does not certify quality or replace human judgment.
