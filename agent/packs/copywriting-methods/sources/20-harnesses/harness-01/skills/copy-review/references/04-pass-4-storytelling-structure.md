# Pass 4: Storytelling Structure

**Purpose:** Connect the claims into an explanation, not a list. Show why a fact matters when evidence supports the explanation.

**When to use:** After voice matching. Rework the structure without changing the substantiated meaning.

## The rule

A reader should understand how the main idea, evidence and implications connect. Metrics without context are a report, not a story. When the evidence is thin, ask a focused question rather than adding generic prose.

## How to build the narrative

1. **Find the arc.** Establish why the reader should care, the relevant context, the problem or tension, the supported response and the next step.
2. **Connect claims.** Explain the relationship between evidence and conclusion. Do not imply causation where the evidence only shows correlation.
3. **Explain changes.** If a number moved, identify the verified reason. If the reason is unknown, flag it for the person or source that can answer.
4. **Vary structure.** Mix sentence lengths and paragraph shapes. Avoid a uniform sequence of bullets or identically sized paragraphs.
5. **Close the loop.** End with a clear takeaway or appropriate action, if the format calls for one.

## Common structural tells

- Equal-length paragraphs with the same shape.
- A string of bullets with no connection between them.
- A list of metrics without explaining what changed or why it matters.
- Generic filler in place of evidence.
- A hook or call to action that does not follow from the supporting claims.

## Output

A draft with a clear narrative thread, supported explanation and no invented causal links.
