# Five-pass copywriting method

**Purpose:** A repeatable sequence for orienting, researching, drafting, checking voice and shaping copy. This is a method, not a source of facts or project policy.

## Step 0: Orient and gather sources

Before drafting:

1. Read the current brief, approved positioning, voice guidance and any applicable legal or review guidance.
2. Identify the reader, their context, the channel, the goal and any required action.
3. Follow the project's current source hierarchy. Prefer primary, current evidence. Treat cached retrieval as a lead, not as authority.
4. Record missing or conflicting evidence. Do not invent facts or numbers, or resolve a source conflict by guessing.

**No usable brief, no draft.** Ask for the missing direction. If essential guidance is absent, note the gap rather than making up a rule.

## Pass 1: Big idea

Write one sentence that captures the central idea. It should be simple, relevant to the intended reader, distinctive for a substantiated reason, and useful beyond one isolated line. If it is unclear, revise before drafting the rest.

## Pass 2: Factual anchoring

List the factual claims in the draft: numbers, product capabilities, people, dates, comparisons and other verifiable statements. Trace each claim to current evidence and cite it where a reviewer can check it. Mark unsupported claims as `[UNVERIFIED]`; resolve them before voice review. A citation does not make a disallowed claim acceptable.

## Pass 3: Voice matching

Read the current voice guidance and define the intended reader from the brief. Revise for tone, rhythm and word choice. Avoid generic marketing, filler and mismatched register. Read the copy aloud and ask whether it feels precise, respectful and suited to the reader. Keep this judgment distinct from factual or legal review.

## Pass 4: Storytelling structure

Make the copy a connected explanation rather than a list of claims. Give the reader a reason to care, establish the context, connect claims, and show the implication or next step. Vary sentence and paragraph shape. Explain why a figure changed only when evidence supports the explanation; otherwise ask a focused question instead of filling the gap.

## Pass 5: Compliance gate

Check the approved terminology and claims guidance. Re-verify time-sensitive numbers against current sources. Check every quote against its original source and obtain any required permission. Flag legal, regulatory, performance, comparison or forward-looking claims for qualified review under the current project process. Remove unsupported claims, misleading implications and unresolved placeholders. A clean checklist is not approval.

## Format adaptation

After the five passes, adapt the copy to the chosen format and its constraints. Do not let a format change alter the substantiated meaning. Apply current channel, length, accessibility and approval requirements.

## Output

A draft with a one-sentence big idea, traceable claim sources, unresolved questions clearly marked, and notes on format-specific review. Publish or send only after the required human approval.
