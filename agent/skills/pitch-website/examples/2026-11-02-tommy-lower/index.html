How the methods fit together

I group related ways of working into families: research, positioning, copywriting, thought leadership. That lets me keep the methods for a kind of work together, rather than treating each workflow as an isolated set of instructions.

The pitch template below brings several methods together. The threadbot uses questions about current work to draw out material for posts.

The working rules

The advice is meant to come from written methods, rather than a model guessing what I'd say. My corrections are recorded to guide later answers. The same correction twice becomes a fixed rule. The answers can still be wrong.

These are the rules as I wrote them down. In the second one, deterministic means giving repeatable work fixed steps and limits; it doesn't mean scripting the judgment.

  • Nothing is automated before it is explained and estimated.
  • Delegation is deterministic and well scoped.
  • Client-facing output passes me first.
  • Repeatable workflows are written in my format and shown to me before running.
  • Scripts come only after two identical manual runs.
  • Judgment steps are never scripted.

How the methods combine

This pitch template combines research, positioning and copy review. The workpaper example adds the delegation method. Each can also be used on its own.

Research comes before the pitch. I look at who the person is, what the business says it does, and what public evidence supports. For this page, that meant your studio and firm sites, posts and GitHub. The brief separates what has been verified, what a source reports, what is inferred and what is still unknown.

Then positioning: what to say, who it's for and why it would matter to them. For the idea, I try different ways of seeing the problem: change its meaning, look at how people choose, find a cultural tension, use a metaphor or challenge a common assumption. Then Ogilvy's five questions: did it make me gasp? Do I wish I'd thought of it? Is it unique? Does it fit the strategy to perfection? Could it be used for 30 years? Voice, claims and the whole page still need their own review.

The first live run was a coffee shop in El Retiro. Its public repo includes the research brief, my corrections and the resulting page, so you can see what changed along the way.

Questions from the work
