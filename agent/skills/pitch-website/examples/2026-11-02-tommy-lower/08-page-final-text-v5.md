## How the methods fit together

I group related ways of working into families: research, positioning, copywriting, thought leadership. That lets me keep the methods for a kind of work together, rather than treating each workflow as an isolated set of instructions.

The pitch template below brings several methods together. The threadbot uses questions about current work to draw out material for posts.


## The working rules

The advice is meant to come from written methods, rather than a model guessing what I'd say. My corrections are recorded to guide later answers. The same correction twice becomes a fixed rule. The answers can still be wrong.

These are the rules as I wrote them down. In the second one, deterministic means giving repeatable work fixed steps and limits; it doesn't mean scripting the judgment.

- Nothing is automated before it is explained and estimated.
- Delegation is deterministic and well scoped.
- Client-facing output passes me first.
- Repeatable workflows are written in my format and shown to me before running.
- Scripts come only after two identical manual runs.
- Judgment steps are never scripted.

## How the methods combine

This pitch template combines research, positioning and copy review. The workpaper example adds the delegation method. Each can also be used on its own.

Research comes before the pitch. I look at who the person is, what the business says it does, and what public evidence supports. For this page, that meant your studio and firm sites, posts and GitHub. The brief separates what has been verified, what a source reports, what is inferred and what is still unknown.

Then positioning: what to say, who it's for and why it would matter to them. For the idea, I try different ways of seeing the problem: change its meaning, look at how people choose, find a cultural tension, use a metaphor or challenge a common assumption. Then Ogilvy's five questions: did it make me gasp? Do I wish I'd thought of it? Is it unique? Does it fit the strategy to perfection? Could it be used for 30 years? Voice, claims and the whole page still need their own review.

The first live run was a coffee shop in El Retiro. Its public repo includes the research brief, my corrections and the resulting page, so you can see what changed along the way.


## One method on a Soteris task

Take drafting a workpaper. The ladder starts with a person doing the work while the agent records it. At later stages, more of the preparation can run from written steps and tested rules. Progress depends on evidence across the whole task: its weakest part limits how far it can go.

An illustrative example based on Soteris's public pages, not its private workflow. The inputs and errors are examples, not observations of your team.


[Demo interactive element on the page: five rung buttons swap the panel below. Rung question and the machine/person roles are always visible; the seven slots sit behind a "Show the seven slots" disclosure.]


### Rung 1 - Describe

Question: What happens, step by step?
The machine may: Watches real runs and writes down the steps, the inputs and the tools.
The person: Does the work. The CPA drafts; nothing runs on its own yet.
Seven slots:
- Before / Strategize: The goal written down: a draft workpaper for professional review.
- Before / Suspect: Last run's problems listed: a missing invoice, a wrong wallet label.
- Before / Anticipate: The inputs named: the trial balance, the chain data, the client's chart.
- The work / Act: The person drafts while the agent records what was done.
- After / React: Errors handled by the person, noted for next time.
- After / Recognize: A person decides whether the draft is good.
- After / Calculate: The person remembers what went wrong.


### Rung 2 - Explain

Question: Why is it done this way?
The machine may: Drafts each step from the written procedure. The person edits every one.
The person: Edits the drafts. The rubric says what good means.
Seven slots:
- Before / Strategize: Why the plan serves the goal, and which constraints shaped it.
- Before / Suspect: Each kind of risk is named, with why it matters.
- Before / Anticipate: Which inputs are mandatory, and why.
- The work / Act: The agent drafts; the CPA edits every step.
- After / React: Known error types have written handling.
- After / Recognize: The agent checks its draft against the quality rubric.
- After / Calculate: There are named categories for what caused a failure.


### Rung 3 - Predict

Question: What changes when the inputs change?
The machine may: Picks the known variant for a known change. Flags the rest and stops.
The person: Reviews every run. Anything outside the known cases stops for review.
Seven slots:
- Before / Strategize: A rule selects the plan for a known input change.
- Before / Suspect: Checks new inputs for contradictions, sensitive data and injected instructions.
- Before / Anticipate: Predicts which checks will fail before the work runs.
- The work / Act: Scripts run the exact steps; the model fills defined parts of a template.
- After / React: Scripts handle known errors; unknown errors stop the work.
- After / Recognize: Predicts whether the reviewer will accept; separate checks still run.
- After / Calculate: Causes get classified; the learning is recorded.


### Rung 4 - Estimate

Question: How long, how much, how risky?
The machine may: Estimates time, cost and risk from past runs. Stays within budget or stops.
The person: Reviews samples and exceptions, not every line.
Seven slots:
- Before / Strategize: Estimates time and cost for each plan from recorded runs, within budget.
- Before / Suspect: Each risk gets a probability from history.
- Before / Anticipate: Past records show failure rates and lead times for checks before the work.
- The work / Act: Scripts do most work; the model fills templates only where the rubric calls for synthesis.
- After / React: Error rates and recovery costs tracked.
- After / Recognize: Checks predicted acceptance against actual outcomes and watches for changes in reliability.
- After / Calculate: Causes tied to cost; the next fix picked by impact.


### Rung 5 - Delegate

Question: What exactly may run on its own?
The machine may: Runs the scripted preparation inside budget. Exceptions and samples reach a person.
The person: Keeps the conclusion. A licensed professional judges and signs; that part doesn't move.
Seven slots:
- Before / Strategize: Uses a plan template and rules for known cases; novel cases follow a declared escalation path.
- Before / Suspect: Uses fixed rules; risks above the threshold follow the escalation path.
- Before / Anticipate: Scripts run the checks before work starts.
- The work / Act: Scripts and templates, within budget; the model combines information only in defined parts.
- After / React: Fixes known problems within set limits; anything beyond them is reported as an incident.
- After / Recognize: The CPA reviews exceptions and a sample of runs.
- After / Calculate: A review of the run proposes the next rule or script change.

In my own process, scripts come only after two identical manual runs. Judgment steps never get scripted. Those are my working rules, not claims about Soteris.

For this example, a licensed professional still judges and signs the conclusion at every stage. Soteris calls its approach agentic, not autonomous. The ladder shows changes in preparation, not a transfer of that sign-off.
