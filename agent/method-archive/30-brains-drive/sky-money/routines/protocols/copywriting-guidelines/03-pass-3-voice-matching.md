# Pass 3: Voice Matching

**Purpose:** Ensure the tone, rhythm, and word choice align with `brand-voice.md`. The copy should sound like this brand, not a generic marketing template.

**When to use:** After Pass 2 (Factual Anchoring) is complete. Revise the draft for voice alignment.

---

## The Rule

Read the draft aloud. Does it sound like this brand? If it sounds like generic marketing copy, rewrite.

---

## Brand Voice Principles

### For Both Brands

- **Direct, not fluffy.** No filler, no hype, no emoji, no motivational language.
- **Specific, not vague.** Use concrete numbers and examples, not generalizations.
- **Confident, not defensive.** State facts plainly. Do not over-explain or justify.
- **Professional, not casual.** Match the density of the audience. Institutional allocators expect precision; DeFi natives expect technical accuracy.

### the client product Specific

- **Audience:** DeFi-native yield seekers, crypto-native startup CFOs
- **Tone:** Accessible but technical. Assume high crypto literacy.
- **Tagline:** "Put your stablecoins to work."
- **Key voice reference:** `../../memory/strategy/concepts/brand-voice.md`

### the client Specific

- **Audience:** Institutional allocators, governance participants, agents
- **Tone:** Institutional-grade. Every sentence must pass the "review standard" (see Pass 5).
- **Tagline:** "Where Capital Never Sits Still."
- **Key voice reference:** `../../memory/strategy/concepts/brand-voice.md`
- **Institutional messaging:** `../../memory/strategy/concepts/institutional-messaging.md`

---

## How to Match Voice

### Step 1: Read Brand Voice Guidelines

Read `../../memory/strategy/concepts/brand-voice.md` before revising the draft. Note:
- Approved words and phrases
- Banned words and phrases
- Tone guidelines
- Audience expectations

### Step 2: Revise for Tone

Go through the draft and revise sentences that:
- Sound like generic marketing copy ("leveraging synergies," "unlocking value")
- Use banned words (see brand-voice.md and financial-terminology.md)
- Are too casual for the audience (the client product can be more conversational; the client Eco must be institutional)
- Are too dense for the audience (don't over-explain DeFi basics to crypto natives)

### Step 3: Read Aloud

Read the draft aloud. Ask:
- Does this sound like something a stakeholder would write?
- Does this sound like something the author would approve?
- Would a reviewer be comfortable reading this aloud to a billion-dollar institution?

If the answer to any question is "no," revise.

---

## Common Voice Mistakes

- **AI filler phrases:** "demonstrating," "underscoring," "continued momentum," "strong fundamentals," "leveraging," "harnessing," "key metrics" with no specifics
- **Motivational language:** "exciting," "thrilled," "proud to announce," "game-changer"
- **Hedge words:** "we believe," "we think," "potentially," "may be" (unless expressing genuine uncertainty)
- **Over-explanation:** Explaining DeFi basics to an audience that already knows them
- **Under-explanation:** Assuming institutional allocators know the client-specific terminology they don't

---

## Output of Pass 3

A revised draft that matches the brand's voice — direct, specific, no AI filler, no motivational language, appropriate tone for the audience.
