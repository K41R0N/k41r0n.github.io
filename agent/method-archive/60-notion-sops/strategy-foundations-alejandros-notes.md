# Page content (verbatim Notion-flavored Markdown)

Whenever there’s a task I know I’m gonna have to do more than once, I plan out the step-by-step of how I would do it manually. For example, if I knew I had to research context about a client, I’d probably go at it like:

<callout icon="💡" color="gray_bg">
<b>Step 1: </b>Map out what kind of information I <i>need</i> to know <i>(what’s the website, who is their team, have they ever been on the cover of TIME, what are we actually working with?)</i>

<b>Step 2: </b>Map out the second degree questions I’ll need to land on the objectives <i>(Who is their competition, how saturated is their market segment, what have they done and said before and did it work?)</i>

<b>Step 3: </b>How do all of these questions’ answers add up to a general direction for the brand?
</callout>

<p />

After I’ve outlined how <i>I’d </i>do it, I can then start thinking about how an agent could fit into this. In the above example, this same line of questioning became the CANs framework I use for researching strategy.

Some exaples of the tools I’ve built using this structure are below, though I try to make it happen with everything I do repeteadly.
