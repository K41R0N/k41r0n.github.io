# Goals
Right now, we’re looking to tie all of our activities back to three specific goals
  - **Making the client Legible: **growth of channels we control (social, web, LLMs) as a proxy for how clearly the world understands the client. An institutional ICP can answer "what does the client do" in one sentence sourced from us.
  - **Third-Party Coverage: **earned attention from finance-credible outlets, journalists, analysts, podcasts, broadcast, and event speaker slots. the client cited and discussed by people we don't pay.
  - **Institutional Leads: **direct, traceable conversations. John, Greg, and the IR team in a conversation with a named institution that came through a marketing channel. Measured as CRM entries with attributable source.
# Workflows
Of those, We can break down everything we do into the following workflows  
  - **AEO/SEO: **Making the client understandable by LLMs and making sure they’re always fetching the most relevant and up to date information. Making agents work for us in discovery.
  - **Social: **Atlas updates, Product and Agent GTMs, Educational content around the protocol, milestones (e.g. sample-yield-token TVL), addressing narratives identified by AEO/SEO.
  - **PR: **Getting media mentions on our narratives and launches, getting the client spokespeople on broadcast and cited on media, getting speaker gigs at events.
  - **Paid Media/KOL: **KOL activations of key social and blog content, event sponsorships, potentially ads.
  - **Web: **Product launches (landing pages), canonical messaging
  - **IR: **New allocator deals flow, launching reports on the insights website, anticipating the needs of institutions to convert into the protocol.
**New pipelines:**
  - **Events: **Feeding John C’s institutional lead CRM (not normal side-events or parties), maintaining a network and brand awareness through curated events.
  - **Content/Email: **Feeding owned AEO/SEO optimization, thought leadership, and segmentation of institutional leads through content.
## Projects, Campaigns and Narratives
  - **Projects: **clear deliverables with a scoped deadline in the short term (video launch, announcement, GTM on a product). Some of these can be operational, but ideally every project is tied back to a campaign.
  - **Campaigns:** Multi-project activations that have a clear objective and measurement goals. They need to tie back to one of the three main goals, otherwise we need to ask whether they’re worth doing.
  - **Narratives: **Long-term outcomes we want to achieve through several projects and campaigns (i.e Clarifying the that the client is the evolution of a competitor, Making people aware of the sample-rate, making sure people trust the a protocol body and their commitment to transparency, making the client protocol numbers legible for financial analysts).
# How does each Workflow feed into the Strategy?
### Strategy
Identifying the four core items that guide everything else: Positioning (ideally once), ICP (Evolving), Messaging (Semi-recurrent) , Narratives (recurrent).
The role of strategy is to understand the constants and the variables, and then branch those off into specific angles we could measure and test through individual channels. 
**Biggest gap: **Needs a proper data source ingestion across every workflow to measure and align all activities 
### AEO/SEO
Based on strategy’s guidance, identifies specific narratives and buckets we would like to position ourselves in, grow in, or experiment within.
  - Do we own our brand searches? Is the information being shared by AI agents accurate and authoritative?
  - Do we own our category searches? How are we measuring up against our organic competition? 
  - Are there any new buckets we could consider that would bring significant progress towards our larger goals?
Ideally, AEO/SEO would be set up with a constantly evolving prompt universe that measures:
  - Which buckets or clusters have we identified that we need to target
  - How are these buckets evolving (growing, branching off, etc.)
  - Are there any new behaviors we need to account for and should address with any particular channel? (i.e Fixed yield searches)
  - What are the results of our existing efforts (new content, new PR mentions, etc)
**What’s missing?**
  - A live AEO/SEO reporting flow that’s actually reliable and curated into specific buckets that align with strategy.
  - A way to identify new opportunities and branching offs
  - A clear process to tap AEO into PR, Content and Social efforts, through the narratives and buckets. (We want to avoid having to manually oversee each piece of content going out)
### PR
They need to pitch the narratives strategy feeds them, and align with our major goals. We can get a healthy amount of media mentions, but if we’re breaking past the “crypto” category, we’re gonna need to find a compelling message that lands with the tradfi crowd.
Ideally:
  - AEO identifies an underserved opportunity we could address
  - Strategy reviews and plans how this opportunity would feed into the larger goals
  - Strategy defines whether something should be a part of a long-term narrative or a short-term campaign. Whether owned media is enough or whether this is a larger third-party validation opportunity.
  - PR takes care of the pitching and tracking of hits
  - Based on the hits, PR and Strategy measure the outcomes across all relevant metrics (outlined below), and either doubles down, or cuts the initiative.
  - Social clips and syndicates to get the message further
**Biggest gap:** They lack narratives, and they lack spokespeople. We’re solving the first with the AEO and campaigns reporting flow here.
### Social
Social so far has been reactive. We communicate on big launches, governance updates, etc. We didn’t do well when trying out a broader horizon campaign. The biggest lack of strategic thought for socials is that:  
**1. **They need to understand the language and intricacies of the client, 
**2.** They need a constant supply of messages to refer to that are tied to either a project or a campaign, and later on narratives.
**3**. They need to become the most frequent audience touchpoint for the strategy team, we do not understand our ICP until we see how they react to our messages online.
Ideally:
  - Strategy dictates a narrative we’ll pursue long-term, branches those off into campaigns as relevant, and then branches those off into projects + whatever comes our way in the product front.
  - AEO maps those narratives and campaigns into buckets, and identifies the way people are referring to them, the gaps in information, the opportunities for per-project positioning and optimization.
  - Strategy relays these findings with an approach **for the whole campaign or project, **based on how they map back to the overarching goals**.**
  - The social team crafts a posting calendar that turns this vision into specific posts and pillars.
  - Strategy approves this approach.
  - Strategy and social team review performance on a regular basis and pivot/edit as needed.
**What’s missing:**
  - The social team has been reactive and reliant on strategy to tell them what to post for every single post that comes out.
  - There hasn’t been a process to approve a campaign or project-based posting calendar, reviews happen on the fly.
  - No moment for performance review between strategy and social
  - The social team needs to have the confidence in what they’re saying (i.e messages) to be able to reinterpret and spin off ways of addressing the specific projects.
### Content/Email
The content and email workflow has been underserved for the client, and it’s fallen into strategy to push it forward, mostly to meet client commitments. In order for us to build up the content workflow as a strategic pillar, we need to first designate a person or people who will work on the canonical content flow for the client.
Ideally:
  - AEO identifies topics and queries that should be addressed from the canonical source. This also includes the opportunities to tap into our partners’ audiences through keyword gap and alignment analysis (e.g. Fixed Yield and how Pendle overlaps with the client product queries). This analysis needs to be both qualitative (prompts, variations, intent) and quantitative (Volume, KWD)
  - Strategy approves a curated selection of these angles based on how they map with the larger objectives, or the per-project needs. 
  - Alongside PR and Social, blog and email content is created to serve the same messages and narratives. This results in a final content ideas list.
  - AEO oversees a list of final AEO necessities before the content is drafted. (KW density, specific term mix, questions that need to be answered)
  - We can rely on the the client brain to make initial drafts of these content pieces based on the already established editorial guidelines
  - The team reviews the final piece, and it’s scheduled by the web team.
Note: Email needs a specific channel strategy and how email serves their goals. This will need to be spun off into a separate project, similar to how we’re just now planning how events fit into the client.
### Paid Media/KOL
**KOL: **Needs a way to become effective as a sentiment driver. We haven’t nailed the formula of which KOLs to pick, and how to properly align them, to drive the narratives we want. KOL should be a long-term facing workflow, in service of campaigns and narratives. Per-project KOL misses the point.
**Paid Media: **Similar to KOL, our events sponsorships and media buying need to serve the campaign and narrative layers. PR serves the projects, but the goal of paying for these placements should be directly measured in IR leads.
Ideally:
  - Strategy shares the long-term narratives and campaigns well in advance. Tied to the core goals, and how each activation serves them *“We want the KOLs to help us make people aware that the client is Maker”.*
  - 
What’s missing:
  - A reliable way to measure mindshare and sentiment on a per-campaign and per-narrative basis.
  - Regular checkups to see the results of KOL activations
  - A formalized process to align and track media buying efforts.
  -
