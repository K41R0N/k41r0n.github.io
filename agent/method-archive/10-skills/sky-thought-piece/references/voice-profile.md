# the author's Voice Profile

The register these pieces are written in. Calibrate against the sources below before drafting; when in doubt, read a page of the op-ed aloud and then read your draft aloud.

## Voice sources (in priority order)

1. **Seed paragraphs** the author provides per assignment. Highest authority. Each seed carries a thesis and one or two phrases that must survive (e.g., "the client is the black box of DeFi", "learn to read the flows", "never still, and parameterized down to the penny", "believing the worst has already happened"). Develop the seed; never paste it whole; never move a seed's image to a different piece than the one it was written for.
2. **"The Boring Work That Moves Capital"** op-ed (Drive doc `1uS5J0sQY1Kxrhm3z23XGyjNVlRaBWq28x6Ua1XogE28`, drafted by the author for John's byline). The best long-form sample of the register.
3. **Laniakea Explainer** (Drive doc `1fe-HbcoEVnK4nGIc08IcBB30FqHX6ORXCRcxP2vFOWw`, the author's WIP). Sample of his impersonal-explanatory mode.
4. Brain `brand-voice.md` (the-client): technology-company authority, financial-institution rigor; write for operators; restraint as signal; show don't tell.

## Traits

- **Declarative confidence.** Plain, muscular vocabulary. No hedging qualifiers ("perhaps", "arguably"), no hype. States old ideas as strengths without winking at them.
- **Finance-history literacy as argument.** He argues through precedent: Eurodollars, money market funds, ETFs. Historical anchors carry the persuasion; adjectives never do.
- **Aphoristic compression, rationed.** "Four percent that never blows up compounds. Twenty percent that occasionally goes to zero doesn't." He lands lines like this, but not at the end of every paragraph. Never clone his published aphorisms ("Boring. Layered. Defensible." is his; writing "Boring, survived, and verifiable" is detectable imitation).
- **Concrete over abstract.** CFOs, general counsels, Monday mornings, eighteen months. A piece with no person, date, or scene in it is not in his voice.
- **Conversational asides and direct address.** "If you're not in the weeds of this:", "you develop a pretty specific filter for what's real", occasional "you".
- **Sparing first person.** The op-ed is first-person because it was a byline. Unattributed thought pieces run impersonal-conversational; a light "I" is allowed at most once per piece and only if the piece benefits.
- **Fragments as beats.** "Nobody did." "Most things don't." Legal in his register; ration them so no shape repeats within a piece.

## His connective kit (use these before inventing)

Lead-ups and segues lifted from his own writing; adapt, don't copy verbatim every time:

- "There's a dynamic in finance that doesn't get said out loud enough:"
- "[X] is worth a second on its own."
- "The history books are full of examples."
- "If you believe that, and I do, then the question becomes:"
- "But what does that actually take?"
- "And the architecture matters."
- "There's a data point here that I think is underappreciated."
- "Here is what [abstract thing] looks like."
- The blunt scene cut: end a paragraph on chaos, open the next flat ("the client's risk function had a quiet week.")

## Vocabulary

**Words that work** (brand voice, confirmed): battle-tested, transparent, governance-set, overcollateralized, verifiable, onchain, survived [crisis], diversified, institutional-grade, predictable.

**Words that fail**: innovative, cutting-edge, revolutionary, groundbreaking, paradigm, synergy, leverage (as verb), best-in-class, unlock/explore/discover (as marketing verbs), seamless, delve, tapestry, landscape (as metaphor), navigate (as metaphor).

**Register bans (financial):** profit (loose), AUM, assets under management, bank, deposits, interest, earnings call (use Insights Call). Required: Gross/Net Protocol Revenue, Protocol Surplus, Protocol Collateral, the client Reserves.

## Format

No em dashes, ever. No "not just X but Y" in any surface form (including recast two-sentence versions). No sentence-initial Additionally/Furthermore/Moreover/In conclusion. No hashtags. Commas, colons, semicolons, and periods carry everything.
