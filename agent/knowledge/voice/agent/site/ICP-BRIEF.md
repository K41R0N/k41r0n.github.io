# ICP brief for the site rewrite (draft)

Status: proposal. Built from three sources, each with its limits.

## What we know from his own material (internal)
- Target in his words (client-finding workstream, Oct 28): medium and service businesses, agencies, content-heavy teams. Pitch: a product studio that helps them adopt technology "in a more intuitive, less technical, more human way", built on an agent fleet. A direction he was shown but pushed to ground in his theses: "software you own, built for the team you have", a governed marketing brain the client owns. Treat that line as unconfirmed.
- ICP harness method (his): ICP is a named list of people, never a segment. So this brief describes patterns in the list, not a persona.

## What the ICP watchlist shows (read-only, aggregate only)
Canonical CRM sheet, 200 rows, read Oct 29. Individuals deliberately not named here.
- Fit tags: Marketing strategy 164, Digital product development 129, AI marketing 86, Martech 77.
- Titles (keyword counts, overlap possible): founder/co-founder 78, CTO 43, marketing 39, director 36, head-of 31, product 29, CEO 22, partner 20, growth 18.
- So the list skews to founders and senior operators at small AI and product companies, plus marketing and growth leads. Few large-enterprise CMOs. This differs from "medium businesses and agencies" in his stated target. Worth his call on which group the site speaks to first.
- Caveat: fit tags and "why they matter" text were generated by an earlier agent, not by him. Notes column was empty at last check.

## What external sources say about buyers (public, read Oct 29)
- Brand voice is the live pain. Survey coverage: more than half of marketers say AI captures facts but misses emotional tone, and about one in three is highly confident AI preserves brand identity (martech.org, https://martech.org/ai-adoption-is-outpacing-marketings-ability-to-manage-it/). A survey says 25% of marketers knowingly publish off-brand AI content under deadline (Optimizely via https://cmotech.news/story/optimizely-study-finds-marketers-cut-corners-on-ai).
- Managing AI output eats the time AI saves (same martech.org piece).
- Most organizations are early: 83% in early stages of embedding AI in marketing, per ACAM and Kantar (https://production.marketing-interactive.com/marketing-ai-australia-centre-marketing-acam-kantar).
- Search is shifting: a Typeface survey of 500+ US and UK marketing leaders (Oct 14, 2026) says 49% plan to deprioritize traditional SEO, with concern that chasing AI visibility erodes authenticity (https://ppc.land/49-of-marketers-plan-to-deprioritize-seo-typeface-survey-finds/, https://www.cmswire.com/the-wire/the-ai-visibility-gap-brands-are-trading-away-the-authenticity/). These are vendor surveys, not neutral studies.
- How buyers vet an AI advisor: proof of a workflow shipped in the advisor's own business, a written quality gate, a named in-house owner per workflow, not a strategy deck (https://www.webtonic.io/blog/ai-marketing-advisory-how-to-choose-a-provider, https://www.brianfidler.com/insights/do-you-need-an-ai-marketing-consultant, https://arsum.com/blog/posts/ai-marketing-consulting/). These are consultancies' own marketing pages, so they show the language buyers are being taught, not proven buying behavior.
- AEO basics for a personal site, from practitioner posts: allow AI crawlers in robots.txt, sitemap, llms.txt, JSON-LD (Person, WebSite, BlogPosting), answer-first sections, canonical URLs and OG images (https://www.davidhoang.com/writing/optimizing-for-agents, https://agnelnieves.com/blog/optimizing-a-personal-site-for-ai-search-in-2026, https://emasuriano.com/blog/2026-08-31-making-a-personal-site-readable-to-llms/). Practitioner opinion, not a standard.

## Gaps still open (need him or more research)
1. Who the site speaks to first: founders of small AI/product companies (what the list shows) or medium and service businesses (what he said).
2. Real buyer language. Everything above is trade press and vendor pages. His interview answers and conversations with people on the list are better sources and are not in this brief.
3. What he sells now, in his words. Studio Stupid offerings PDF and the marketing harness repo are not read by me for this pass.
4. Which proof he can show publicly. Client work is confidential.

## What this means for rewrites (hypotheses, not decisions)
- Lead with his own proof: the harnesses and methods he built and uses, shown as method, not as client results.
- Name the pain in buyers' words: AI output that is correct but off-voice, and the time spent managing it.
- Show the quality gate as a visible asset: his edit-rate and sealed-prediction approach, and the rule that checks fail output but never certify voice.
- Keep his plain, anti-buzzword voice. Rewrites carry placeholders where his take is missing.
