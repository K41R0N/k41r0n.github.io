# Feedback and review method

Treat review as diagnosis and evidence, not a vote or a command to rewrite. The goal is to find the smallest complete change that fixes the underlying issue without losing valid work.

## Diagnose the feedback

| Feedback | First move |
|---|---|
| Wrong premise or framing | Reopen the brief and restructure the argument before line editing. |
| Missing requirement, compliance issue, or changed terminology | Check the authoritative rule, then sweep every affected section or deliverable. |
| Too long | Remove low-value material first; do not recast the whole draft by default. |
| Factual concern | Retrieve the primary source and verify date, scope, and definition. Correct the record if the source supports the correction. |
| Awkward line or lede | Offer a few distinct, meaning-preserving alternatives when a choice is needed. |
| Generic, robotic, or unconvincing | Inspect argument, sequence, paragraph development, rhythm, and evidence; word swaps alone rarely address the cause. |
| Preference with no stated reason | Ask what outcome or reader problem the correction is meant to solve if the answer changes the work. |

## Review in separate lenses

For substantial work, separate checks because each catches different failures:

1. Mechanical requirements and source checks.
2. A complete self-edit for clarity, redundancy, and internal consistency.
3. An independent review from a fresh reader, using named lenses such as argument, audience, evidence, structure, and voice.
4. Adjudication of each material finding: accept, adapt, or decline with a reason.
5. A verification review that checks the specific fixes and searches for errors the revision introduced.
6. A final read of the complete artifact, not just changed lines.

Scale the sequence to the stakes, but keep the purpose of each retained stage distinct. The source method maps a short comment to mechanical check, self-edit, and measured subtraction; a standard post adds independent review and a full read; an article, correction, or red-line piece uses the full sequence, including verification and a final knock-on read. Do not claim an independent review or verification pass if it did not happen. A consequential report or long-form piece needs distinct factual, structural, and audience checks.

## Adjudicate rather than obey blindly

- Verify factual critiques against a source. Reviewers can misread context or attribute material incorrectly.
- Preserve explicit constraints and supplied source material unless the operator changes them. Surface a conflict rather than silently overruling either source.
- Fix a recurring class of error across the whole affected set, not only the example quoted in the review.
- Do not accept every stylistic suggestion if it weakens clarity or makes the writing generic. Keep the strongest version and explain consequential judgment calls.
- Record substantive decisions and unresolved issues in a compact change note when the work needs a review trail.

## Correction and evidence record

For a factual correction, retrieve the authoritative source before asking the operator to repeat context. State the discrepancy, the source checked, the proposed correction, and which other artifacts need the same correction. For terminology or compliance, search the affected set and keep one current reference that future briefs can use; burying a changed label in a thread is a known failure mode. Record unresolved items with an owner or next evidence needed instead of implying they were settled.

## Review packet and disposition record

For an independent review, give the reviewer the brief, complete artifact, source material needed to check claims, audience, constraints, and the specific review lenses. Ask for ranked findings with exact quoted spans, why each matters, a concrete proposed fix, and what evidence would settle it. The reviewer reports findings, not approvals.

Use one row per material finding:

| ID | Rank | Lens | Exact span | Issue and evidence | Proposed change | Disposition (accept / adapt / decline) + reason | Verification needed | State |

After revision, have a fresh verifier check explicit pass/fail statements for each accepted or adapted finding, inspect for new problems introduced by the edits, then read the complete final artifact. Keep factual verification distinct from style/structure review. A clean checklist does not mean approved.

## Exit test

The review is ready to close when the main argument is supported, required constraints hold, material findings have dispositions, and the final pass finds no new substantive issue. A clean mechanical check establishes only what it tests; the accountable human decides whether the result is acceptable.
