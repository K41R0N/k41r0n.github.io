# Reader-specific pitch page: review learnings, 2026-10-31

## Purpose and status

This is a dated record of a reader-specific explanation of audience-research and marketing methods. It records choices and review learnings, not a universal positioning statement, a validated offer, or proof of business results.

## What the page had to explain

The page uses two distinct method libraries as examples of one broader, composable approach:

- Audience research records ways to listen and monitor for changes as an audience moves through a funnel.
- Marketing strategy and positioning look at the market, alternatives, offer, and what can make an offering marketable.
- These are distinct bodies of methods, not a claim that every agent is specialized in only one task. A capable agent can draw on more than one method library and should know which source fits the present task.
- Start with the reader’s decision and the specific way the method could help; do not lead with infrastructure.

Explain the work through an actual, bounded example. Show the workflow and how a staged attention framework can determine what is ready for more delegation. Do not imply that an illustrative workflow is already the reader’s process, has been deployed for their accounts, or has produced outcomes for their business.

## Review decisions that improved the explanation

1. **Lead with a real use, not infrastructure.** Too much opening detail about repositories and providers delayed the answer to what the methods do and why the reader should care. Start with the practical process and its value to the reader; explain portability after the work is concrete.
2. **Name the example as an example.** The earlier exposition sounded like a promise, while the body described the author’s own use. Label the example plainly and distinguish it from a live deployment.
3. **Show the attention framework in its own section.** Explain the five depth rungs in clear steps and connect them to a real task: learn what happens, why, what should happen in known cases, how much time/cost it takes, then consider bounded delegation. Explain the seven slots around the task (Strategize, Suspect, Anticipate, Act, React, Recognize, Calculate), rather than gesturing at the framework. Do not imply that tasks can skip the evidence gates or that the page itself evaluates the reader's work.
4. **Explain method library types from the canonical standard, not memory.** The named method-library types come from a canonical standard. Read it before using them; do not invent additional types from an illustrative use case.
5. **Explain composition without overselling portability.** An agent may draw from multiple method sources. A repository is a human-readable, owner-controlled source of methods and context, while AI surfaces and integrations differ. Portability is a design goal, not a guarantee that every provider or setup works without adaptation.
6. **Make the offering and UVP specific, but evidence-bounded.** State what a method or example enables, who it is for, and what distinguishes the approach. Replace adjectives and general capability claims with steps, artifacts, and sources the reader can inspect. Do not turn a proposed test, capability, or market hypothesis into an established result.
7. **Customize from relevant sources.** Use the reader's actual context and relevant source material. Clearly separate sourced facts, owner-reported experience, hypotheses, and unknowns. Do not expose other clients' confidential facts or private source material.
8. **Use the design method and existing brand system.** The design pass used Impeccable with the site's own `design.md` as the palette and type source, not Impeccable's sample palette. Keep the colors coherent, remove unnecessary decoration, and inspect the live result at desktop and mobile sizes.
9. **Teach through a worked example.** The owner preferred an interactive, step-by-step presentation that makes each rung and interview round visible and demonstrates a rule changing the output. Use interaction only when it teaches; verify every control and label the example as illustrative.
10. **Review the whole argument after each substantive correction.** When feedback reveals a framing mismatch, revisit the opening and section order, not only the sentence named. Check consistency between the promise, example, offering, and next step. The page's visual quality is not a substitute for a truthful claim or a clear reader fit.

## Evidence and limits
