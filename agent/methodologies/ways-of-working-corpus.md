# The ways-of-working corpus

What this is: the reference the explainer service answers from. Source: his own ways-of-working compendium (142 methods from his Impact work, received 2026-10-29), distilled so anyone who asks him a question gets his actual approach in plain words - the root why, the problem it kills, and the easy way to start. Client names, figures and vocabulary stay out; the method is his, the instances are not. Where a method has a pack in the methods library, the pack is the executable version and this is the explanation.

## When someone asks: how do I stop AI from making things up?

**Never invent; silence is valid.** The root: a confident wrong answer destroys trust faster than no answer ever will. His line: a missing number reported honestly costs a minute; a wrong number reported confidently costs the account. Apply it by marking every gap as a gap - [TBD], [VERIFY], "not stated" - and shipping anyway. A document with gaps marked as questions beats one filled with plausible prose. And when the deadline pressures you to fill: the deadline is wrong, not the document.

**Every claim carries where it came from.** The root: memory is not a source. Numbers travel with their source, date and surface or they do not ship; anything older than a day gets re-fetched, never recalled. Apply it by labeling every claim as you write it: said by the person, confirmed in a document, or inferred. Inferred is never presented as fact.

**Work the source ladder, in order.** The root: not all sources deserve equal trust. His direct statements outrank the playbook, which outranks the notes, which outrank the web. Chat and headlines are discovery surfaces, not authority. Apply it by deciding the order before you need it, and checking the primary source behind any headline before repeating it.

**Stop when the material is thin.** The root: drafting from generalities feels productive and produces nothing. His gate: no stake, no timing, a big claim resting on a weak source, a load-bearing fact that cannot be retrieved, or fewer than two independent sources - any of these means ask one sharp question instead of writing. Pack: advice-answer.

**A cause needs an artifact.** The root: "the number moved because X" is a guess unless you can point at the thing that moved it. Classify the mechanism before searching, match on date and amount, then cite the artifact. Never state a cause from vibes.

**When sources disagree, show all of them.** The root: silently picking one hides the problem from everyone including yourself. Report every value and which one you used; fix the mapping, never the number.

## When someone asks: how do I get agents to do real work reliably?

**Manual first, then automate.** The root: you cannot automate what you have not defined. Whenever a task will happen more than once, he writes the step-by-step he would do by hand, then looks at where an agent fits. Apply it by mapping three things: what information is needed, what second-degree questions reach the objective, how the answers add up to a direction.

**Counting beats judgment.** The root: anything that can be mechanized should be, because a script runs the same way every time and a mood does not. Rules that can be counted become checks with fixtures. But a script is an accelerant, never a dependency - every mechanical check keeps a hand procedure for when the script cannot run.

**Nothing ships as him without his yes.** The root: approval is never tacit. Asking is not authorizing. One accountable person per workflow; consultation is a gate, not a courtesy. Pack: everything in this library.

**Failures become fixtures.** The root: a failure you do not encode will happen again. Every real failure that reaches an output becomes a test that trips exactly that rule, in the same cycle. The test suite is the system's immune memory.

**Corrections cascade and stick.** The root: fixing only the instance in front of you leaves the old fact alive everywhere else. A correction routes to every file carrying it, and the rule keeps the incident that produced it so the reasoning stays checkable. Corrections beat cadence: anything that happened twice gets a fix; anything that blocked output gets a fix first. Never weaken a gate as a fix.

**Two identical manual runs before anything is scripted.** The root: automating a process you have only run once automates your ignorance. Judgment steps are never scripted at all.

**Clean checks never certify quality.** The root: a check proves the countable half - structure, sources, banned patterns. It cannot prove the work is good or sounds like him. His own rejected draft passed every structural check. The verdict a system renders is pass, fix or block; "approved" belongs to a person.

## When someone asks: how do I write copy that does not sound like a robot?

**The tells are architectural, not lexical.** The root: banning words is round one; the real problem is sentence shape, paragraph endings, symmetric triplets, signposting, ring composition. "Reads extremely LLM written" means restructure, not re-word. Apply it by thinking in terms of how paragraphs develop, not which adjectives to swap.

**One big idea, fifteen words or fewer, or you have zero.** The root: a piece that is about two things is about nothing. Written at the top of the draft before anything else.

**Seeds win.** The root: the writer's own paragraphs carry the thesis; an editor builds around them and never overrides them. When he hands over an edited draft, his prose is the spine and the beats are placed around it.

**Review in blind stages, scaled to the stakes.** The root: the person who wrote it cannot see it fresh. A mechanical sweep, a self-edit, an independent adversarial read with named lenses, adjudication, a second fresh reviewer hunting tells the rewrite introduced, and a measured subtraction pass always last - with before and after counts. A comment runs the short version; an article runs all eight stages.

**Numbers first, interpretation second, visibly separated.** The root: the reader should never have to untangle what happened from what you think about it.

## When someone asks: how do I do strategy for a client?

**Frameworks in a fixed order, completely, before synthesis.** The root: cherry-picking the fun parts of a canvas produces the strategy you already had. He runs the full sequence - snapshot, lean canvas, value proposition, ICP, empathy map, positioning, positioning statement, narrative, messaging pyramid, open gaps - and every bullet carries its provenance tag: sourced, inferred, or gap. Pack: strategy-canvas.

**A goal with no measurable result tied to an artifact is dropped.** The root: unmeasurable goals cannot be wrong, so they cannot be right either.

**Describe the mechanism, never the outcome.** The root: outcomes are claims anyone can make and competitors can copy; the mechanism is the part only you can say. Messaging explains how the thing works, never promises what it will do for you.

**Research for persuasion starts with the methodology, not the data.** The root: when you need to move someone's position, decide first what would prove it and what you concede, ranked by what that audience finds persuasive - their own evidence reframed beats any external statistic. Then make the strategy falsifiable: a fixed review date, and if a result comes back muddy, drop the exhibit rather than torture it.

**Who speaks for the company, first.** The root: before positioning anything, name who speaks publicly for it, with ownership. If nobody does, that is the first finding.

## When someone asks: how do I research a person, an audience or a market?

**Fixed instruments, not vibes.** The root: two research runs are only comparable if the instrument is the same. His contact research fills a fixed card - who they are, their company, the communications picture, network value, scores with rationale, confidence, and the gaps to probe directly. A biographical summary is not a substitute.

**Disambiguate the ask before investing.** The root: the surface request is often the wrong one. Read the thread behind an inbound ask before researching it; a minimal record is an implicit request for the full method.

**Listen for change, not volume.** The root: a pile of mentions is noise; what you want is movement. Per surface, write where you look, what counts as signal, what you ignore. Record each hit with date, source, who and what was said. Vocabulary drift - the words an audience adopts or abandons - is the earliest signal there is. Pack: audience-listening.

**Rank by who is talking, never raw views.** The root: reach measures distribution, not trust. His video research scores relevance, authority, engagement and format with fixed weights, trusted and blocked lists he maintains, and weights that never change per run - because editing the weights per run makes runs incomparable.

## When someone asks: how do I measure without lying to myself?

**No number without window, surface, unit and caveat.** The root: a number without its frame can be made to say anything. Never sum across platforms; never present one system's measurement as another's truth.

**Three output states, and a shrug is not one.** The root: every question lands answered, answered with a hole (the hole named), or cannot answer with the one-line fix. "I can't tell you that, and here is the one-line fix" is a decision.

**Signal beats impressions.** The root: impressions and engagement are metrics, not KPIs. What predicts outcomes is quality-weighted attention - who paid attention, not how many scrolled past.

**Know your instrument's defects before quoting it.** The root: every analytics setup has known ways it lies. His list runs to eleven: identities not bridged, small samples, silent tagging failures. Apply it by writing down your own instrument's defects before the first number ships.

## When someone asks: how do I run a launch or a campaign?

**Readiness gates the announcement, and the work after the peak is planned before it.** The root: attention decays to near zero within two weeks; if the follow-up is not scheduled, the launch is a spike. His cadence runs from two weeks before to thirty days after, with a baseline measurement on day nine - miss it and you lose the ability to say what changed.

**Judge by signal, plan for the audience split, and say the consumer name once.** The root: most launch failures are coordination failures - unclear names, partner audiences mixed, no baseline, no post-peak plan.

## When someone asks: how do I get a team working the same way?

**No brief, no work.** The root: one page, six questions - what, why, who, when, where, how. Every line earns its place by changing what someone does. Extra detail lives elsewhere. "Never apologize for keeping it brief."

**Problem, root cause, solution - one sentence each.** The root: most stated problems are symptoms. Chase the cause with the reversal test and another why, watching for the false bottoms: "nobody owns it", "no process", "too busy". Interpersonal issues are usually decision-rights issues wearing a costume.

**Extract losslessly, standardize without flattening.** The root: the goal is the version a new hire could run on day 30, not the version the best person runs. Interview, keep the quotes, classify every action: standardize, templatize, or preserve the variant on purpose. Every SOP step is labeled fact, inference or recommendation. "Seniority is not evidence."

**If nothing changes after a post-mortem, it was documentation, not learning.** Five questions in fixed order, time-boxed, written, shared.

## The principles under all of it

Fifteen standing rules recur through everything above. The short version, in his spirit: never invent; provenance on every number; confidence on every claim; sources in a fixed order; compliance before creativity; no brief, no work; house writing rules; surface, never paper over; count what can be counted; manual first; corrections cascade; approval is never tacit; delivery is disciplined; standardize without flattening; repeatable and clean beats fast.
